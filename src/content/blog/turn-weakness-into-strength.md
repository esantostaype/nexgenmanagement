Freddie Mercury is remembered as an extraordinary singer, but his business sense is just as instructive. He built Queen a distinctive identity, scaled the band from local gigs to Live Aid at Wembley, and leveraged partnerships with other major artists. That stand-out mindset translates directly to multifamily management.

## Differences are a source of advantage

Great organizations recognize what makes people and opportunities different and use it. Leaders like Richard Branson, who adapted his management style to work with his dyslexia instead of against it, show that perceived weaknesses can become strengths.

## Five questions to find your blind spots

1. **Do details overwhelm you?** Plan several steps further than you think you need, listing every contingency.
2. **Do you weigh the consequences?** Build consensus with employees, customers and owners before pushing an idea through.
3. **Do you focus on the day-to-day?** Hold regular brainstorms and think one, two and five years ahead.
4. **Are you naturally quiet?** Set time aside to share your thinking with your team, without talking over them.
5. **Do you prefer consistency?** Stay open to changing plans, and explain the reasons when something shifts.

Don't let perceived weaknesses hold you back: recognize them, work on them and use them to help your business grow.
